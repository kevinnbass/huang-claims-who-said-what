# Follow a claim: how the Jensen Huang and Alice Huang claims were stated, by whom, and on what

Open `index.html` (in the repository, the link in the About box opens it as a page). Pick one of the five claims and
see who stated it on which day, how (flatly, with a hedge, disputed or denied), who the statement is put to, what
evidence the sentence offers, which outlets stated it first, and which credit which.

**Status.** This copy holds the 193 documents that all five readers have covered: 151 pages of the four papers, 20 statements and transcripts of the people and bodies themselves, and 22 reports of other outlets. A further 978 articles of other outlets have been coded and are still being checked; they are added when that check is done.

**What is counted.** 1,886 statements in 177 of 193 documents dated Oct 29 to Nov 14, 2026: 151 pages of the
New York Times, the Wall Street Journal, the Financial Times and the Economist; 22 articles and pages of 6 other
outlets and sites; 20 statements and transcripts of the people and bodies themselves. A statement is one sentence,
headline or caption bearing on one claim: that Alice Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset or spy (1,125); that
Jensen Huang had an affair with her (422), with Kimberly Powell (110), with a second Nvidia employee
(10), or an affair with no partner named (219). Counts are statements, not sources: one source quoted in ten
sentences is ten statements. `CODEBOOK.md` defines every field.

**How it was coded.** By automated readers working alone from the codebook, which was fixed before coding. Two coded
every document independently (983 statements stand on their agreement); a third ruled where they differed or only
one had filed (679); a fourth checked statements against their documents and re-read documents for missed
statements; a fifth, the referee, ruled on what the fourth contested and proposed (224 statements changed or added
that way). A script confirms that every coded sentence is in the saved text of its document. No person has checked the
coding line by line.
- Statements the fourth reader checked: 1850 of 12689; documents it re-read: 193 of 1171 (these totals count the whole table, with the articles still being checked).
- Fields it contested: 457 on 249 statements; the referee: for the fourth reader 370; for the table as it stood 70; for a third value 17.
- Statements the re-reads proposed to add: 136; the referee kept 40.

**What this copy leaves out.** Sentences from news articles are not reproduced. For each of the 1,770 statements taken
from an article the table gives only the words that carry the claim (at most eight), who the sentence says is speaking
(cut to eight words where the article's description runs longer), the headline, and a link where the saved page records
one. A sentence coded under two claims is two statements, each with its own words. Counted per article, the words
shown come to about 2% of the article's text at the median and 12% at most (a page of 5,109 characters), at about six
characters a word. The 116 statements from documents issued by the speakers themselves (company, government and
officials' texts) are quoted whole.

**Limits.**
- Coverage is not a census of the press. The four papers are covered as far as their pages could be saved (some of
  their pages are read from republished copies); the other outlets here are a few
  reports saved with them (Bloomberg, The Washington Post, Reuters, CNN, POLITICO, ABC News); a wider set found mostly through
  two news aggregators is still being checked and is not in this copy.
- A document's day is the day it was first published, where its page shows one. The text read is the copy saved, which
  may be a later version: a sentence can be newer than that day. A time of first publication is known for only some pages.
- "Evidence offered" is what the sentence, or the attribution that governs it, names as its basis. It is not a finding
  about what the outlet holds, and a claim put to unnamed people is not thereby false.
- The credited outlet in "who credits whom" is read from the words of the sentence by a fixed list of names.
- Videos and podcasts were not transcribed.
